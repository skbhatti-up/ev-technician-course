Types of electrified vehicle

MHEV, HEV, PHEV, REEV, BEV — what the letters mean and how each one is built.

You will learn

4.1 The electrification ladder

"Electrified" means the car uses an electric motor for at least part of its driving. The more electric it is, the bigger its battery and the higher its voltage.

You may also hear FCEV (fuel-cell electric vehicle). It makes electricity on board from hydrogen gas. There are none in normal use in Pakistan, so this course does not cover them.

In Pakistan

PHEVs suit our conditions well. Most daily city driving is under 50 km, which a PHEV can do on cheap electricity. For Lahore–Islamabad or Karachi–Hyderabad, where chargers are still rare, the petrol engine removes the worry. This is why Chinese PHEV SUVs have sold strongly. Expect to see a lot of them in your workshop.

4.2 Three ways to arrange a hybrid

Every hybrid is one of three basic layouts, or a mixture that can switch between them.

4.3 Reading a specification sheet

When a customer or a parts supplier describes a car, these clues tell you its type:

  • Battery in kWh. About 1–2 kWh → HEV. 10–30 kWh → PHEV. Over 40 kWh → BEV.
  • A charging port → PHEV, REEV or BEV. HEVs and MHEVs have none.
  • An exhaust pipe → it has an engine, so not a BEV.
  • "EV range" and "combined range" quoted together → PHEV or REEV.

4.4 Buses, coaches and trucks

Electric commercial vehicles use the same principles as cars, on a larger scale. Chinese training courses often include a day on them, because China runs the world's largest fleets of electric buses.

Everything in Module 3 applies with more force: higher voltage, far more stored energy, components mounted on the roof (working at height), and a much heavier vehicle.

Summary

  • MHEV → HEV → PHEV → REEV → BEV: battery and voltage grow at each step.
  • HEVs do not plug in. PHEVs, REEVs and BEVs do.
  • Series: engine makes electricity only. Parallel: engine and motor both drive. Series-parallel: both.
  • Battery size in kWh is the quickest clue to the type of car.

Main components: a map of the car

Every major part of an electric car, what it does, and how energy flows between them.

You will learn

  • The names and jobs of the main high-voltage components
  • Where they are usually found in the car
  • How energy flows when charging, driving and braking

5.1 The big picture

Most modern EVs use a "skateboard" layout. The battery is a flat slab under the floor between the axles. The motor and its electronics sit at one or both axles. This keeps the weight low and leaves the cabin free.

5.2 What each component does

Table 5.1 — Main components of an electric car
ComponentJobModule
HV battery packStores the energy (DC, 300–800 V). Contains the cells, the BMS, contactors, fuses and sensors.6–8
BMS (battery management system)The battery's brain. Measures every cell, controls contactors, protects the pack.8
Inverter (motor controller, MCU)Changes battery DC into three-phase AC to drive the motor, and back again during braking.10
Traction motorTurns electrical power into rotation. Also works as a generator when braking.9
Reduction gearA single-speed gearbox with differential. Reduces motor speed and multiplies torque.12
On-board charger (OBC)Changes AC from the mains into DC to charge the battery.10, 14
DC-DC converterSteps HV down to about 14 V to run the 12 V system and charge the 12 V battery. It replaces the alternator.10, 16
PDU (power distribution unit)The high-voltage junction box with fuses and relays for each HV consumer.10
Charge portWhere the charging cable plugs in. AC and DC pins.14
Electric A/C compressorRuns on high voltage, so the air-conditioning works with no engine. Also cools the battery on many cars.15

VCU (vehicle control unit)The master computer. Reads the pedals and decides how much torque to ask from the motor.16

Workshop tip — "x-in-1" units

To save space and cost, makers combine parts into one housing. A "3-in-1" drive unit is motor + inverter + reduction gear. BYD's "8-in-1" adds the charger, DC-DC, PDU, VCU and BMS controller. For a technician this means fewer cables to check — but if one part fails, the whole expensive unit may need to come out.

Key idea — the "three-electric" system

Chinese trainers, manuals and parts suppliers group the main parts under one name: the three-electric system (三电, sān diàn).

  • The "big three": battery (电池), motor (电机) and electronic control (电控: the inverter and vehicle controller).
  • The "small three": on-board charger (OBC), DC-DC converter and power distribution unit (PDU).

You will hear this phrase on the first day of any course in China. It simply means the parts in Table 5.1.

5.3 How energy flows

5.4 How a hybrid differs

A hybrid has all of the above (with a smaller battery in an HEV), plus a complete petrol engine with its fuel, exhaust, cooling and lubrication systems, and a special transmission that combines engine and motor. A hybrid is therefore more complicated than either a petrol car or a pure EV. Module 13 explains hybrids in detail.

Summary

  • Battery under the floor, drive unit at the axle: the "skateboard".
  • Battery (DC) → inverter → motor (3-phase AC) → reduction gear → wheels.
  • Mains AC → on-board charger → battery.
  • The DC-DC converter replaces the alternator and keeps the 12 V battery charged.
  • Braking sends energy back to the battery through the same motor and inverter.
