How to use this course

This course is the first step before hands-on training. It gives you the knowledge you need so that, when you stand in front of a real electric car in a training workshop, you already understand what you are looking at and why the safety rules exist.

You do not need to be an engineer. You need basic maths (multiply, divide, percentages), basic science, and the patience to read carefully.

How each module works

  • You will learn — a short list at the start. Read it first.
  • The lesson — short sections with diagrams. Read slowly. Draw the diagrams yourself in a notebook; drawing helps you remember.
  • Coloured boxes — see the key below.
  • Picture it — purple boxes and drawings that link a new idea to something you already know. These are the easiest way to remember. Close the book and try to see the picture in your mind.
  • Summary — the main points to remember.
  • Quiz — test yourself. Answers are at the back. On the web version you can tap an answer to check it.

The pass mark

Aim for 80% or more in every quiz before moving on. For Module 3 (High-voltage safety) the target is 100%. In safety, one wrong answer is one too many.

Read this before anything else

This book teaches knowledge. It does not make you qualified to work on high-voltage systems. Electric and hybrid cars carry 200 to 800 volts DC. That is enough to kill instantly.

Do not open, disconnect or test any orange cable, battery pack, inverter or motor until you have completed supervised practical training with the correct safety equipment. Until then you may look, read, and learn. Nothing more.

About the vehicle data

Models, prices and specifications in this course were collected in November 2026 from Pakistani motoring press and manufacturer information. Companies change specifications often, and sources sometimes disagree. Figures are given to teach you how to read and compare specifications. Before working on any car, always check the official service information for that exact model and year.
